Robots & IoT

PaperRobot V2 · ESP32 cardboard robot

V2 build demo published · test log pending

Updated from the static V1 OLED page to published V2 integrated firmware and a real build demo: expressions, IR interaction, yaw and touch nod.

ESP32-C3 SuperMini / SSD1306 / 2× SG90 / 2× IR / TTP223 / Arduino C++

Source contains six face designs, blinking and smooth transitions. Left/right IR shifts the whole face and controls yaw; both inputs center it. Touch triggers happiness and one nod; 45 seconds of inactivity selects sleep. The repository records 11 successful sketch compilations on 2026-10-13. The owner reports a completed build and supplied a roughly 74-second demo; the flashed firmware hash is unconfirmed.

Current scope & limitations

Compilation and a demo do not establish a full hardware acceptance test: per-test observations, sustained operation, final power arrangement, exact part ratings, assembly photos and dimensions remain pending. No camera, voice, networking or AI dialogue is implemented. V1 source and documentation are preserved; current V2 remains an inspired adaptation.

Hardware and wiring

One ESP32-C3 SuperMini; one SSD1306 128×64 I²C OLED (0x3C, SDA 0 / SCL 1); two SG90 servos (yaw GPIO4, pitch GPIO5); two IR modules (GPIO6/7); one TTP223 (GPIO10). A 5V UBEC powers servos with common ground; 12V goes only to its input. Existing large breadboard and hand-measured 3mm corrugated cardboard shell; no cutting template.

Assembly and diagnostics

Documented sequence: test USB, OLED, IR, touch and servos separately; cut cardboard to measured parts; center at 90° without horns, then attach with power off; preserve service access and cable slack. Check power and grounding, upload V2 unloaded, then add load gradually. Arduino target ESP32C3 Dev Module, serial 115200. This is a reconstruction guide; assembly photos and the actual build log are pending.

Technical challenges

OLED ACK proves a bus response, not visible pixels: scan the address, then test the display separately. Inputs use 40ms debounce; touch requires release/re-touch and a 2200ms cooldown. Servos move at most 1° per 20ms, with yaw 80–100° and pitch 82–98° defaults; software limits do not establish mechanical safety. Jitter or resets require checks for voltage droop, grounding, contacts and stalls.
